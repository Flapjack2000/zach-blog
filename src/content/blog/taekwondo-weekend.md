I enjoy practicing taekwondo, but I have great difficulty feeling proud of myself for improving at it. In my mind, improvement is simply what I am supposed to do. However, I have no problem feeling proud of other people when they improve and I feel good when can help the students get better.

Teaching is the fun part of taekwondo. We got two new students this month, two young girls, and it's been fun to teach them the basics. I was getting used to working with the high belts.

Last night was the ceremonial black belt test. Every new black belt that tested this past year tested again, with much lower stakes since we already tested. Then we got our new belts. I tested in April in Kentucky, but I'm officially a second degree now.

I don't really care about my rank at all. I think of my rank as a teaching tool. It gives me authority. I'm proud of reaching black belt, but being higher rank than a first degree doesn't matter to me. I think reaching second degree is so much easier than getting to first anyway. You're already a black belt and you have a much better idea of how the organization works. Or at least I do because I help my instructor a lot and she tells me things. I'm not sure about the experiences of other second degrees. I felt a lot more confidence going into my second degree test than going into my first degree test.

Same thing for medals. Today was the fall tournament. I earned a gold medal for my form. It's going in the plastic bag in my closet with the rest. I've only kept them because I think I'm supposed to. As if someone would check that I had them. Not that I didn't do a good job on my form. I think I did really well and better than the others, I just don't feel like I need the recognition. I think it comes from the same part of me that doesn't enjoy receiving compliments.

I sparred twice as well, winning both matches. They were exhibition matches. One of the guys didn't have another person in his age bracket so he needed a partner. The other guy only had his classmate in his bracket, so he wanted to spar somebody else just for fun. I like both those guys, so I obliged. I was surprised I won both. The last time I sparred at a tournament was two years ago, and I lost. Apparently I'm a lot better these days even without doing any sparring practice. I think it just comes down to my speed and accuracy. The last year or so I've only been doing accuracy and speed practice at home, and now I'm able to quickly and accurately kick toward any target at will.
